LexiForge — Keyterm Health for Gnani Prisma v3.0

Gnani gives you 200 keyterm slots per call; LexiForge measures what a candidate list does on your own calls — and proves whether it helped or hurt.

Problem

  • Prisma v3.0 accepts up to 200 keyterms, but the business glossary is far larger and changes. Someone must choose the subset — manually, and usually once.
  • Vendor benchmarks show relevant keyterms help on public clips. They do not tell a customer whether a list helps on their noisy, code-mixed calls, or whether it degrades ordinary recognition.
  • A wrong list is a silent failure: missed brand/product/scheme names break search, analytics and compliance; over-broad lists can displace known words.

What we built

A Hinglish-banking Keyterm Health service: takes a glossary, call audio and reference transcripts; selects a candidate list; runs six arms on the same audio — baseline, selected, mismatched, hard-negative, oracle-200, static-head-200; scores term recall, insertions, WER and paired deltas; returns a report with an A/B verdict and provenance.

A = critical terms (brand, product, must-have). B = all-real guardrail: keyterms must not wreck ordinary recognition.

Evidence — synthetic 4-call pilot

The selected arm was brand-only, 8–9 terms — not a 200-term list. "Fictional-name recall" means invented glossary names, not real bank brands. Output format: transliterate.

Full six-arm table: docs/pilot4-results.md. Internal harness validation — synthetic TTS, not customer evidence.

  • H1 (mismatched < baseline real recall): −17.5pp, p = 0.062 — not rejected.
  • H2 (mismatched > baseline insertions): +0.10/utt, p = 0.503 — not rejected.
  • Pilot verdict (selected vs baseline, B guardrail): real recall −22.5pp, CI [−36.7, −8.3] — FAIL under the −5pp margin.
  • Positive control: oracle − mismatched +9.17pp, CI [0.0, +18.3] — direction consistent, CI touches zero.
  • At n=4 the smallest one-sided sign-flip p is 0.0625; descriptive only.

Key finding: every keyterm list lowered real recall versus baseline — including the oracle — while fictional-name recall moved as intended (16.7% → 83.3–100%). Open question: ASR bias or romanisation drift? The per-term diff decides. Gnani has not measured transliterated-output accuracy, and the matcher has no transliteration; Hindi terms rely on pre-written romanised aliases.

Why Gnani

Gnani sells the 200 slots and the model. LexiForge measures what a list does on the customer's own audio: a POC accelerator, a glossary guardrail, and a measured capability for the keyterm feature Gnani already ships. Deployment alongside the customer's stack is a roadmap item; the pilot ran locally.

Status and limits

  • Working end to end: Gnani REST + Batch clients, Timbre TTS, six arms, report, recording pack, 20 consistent scripts, 841 tests, 47-case matcher gold set.
  • Synthetic TTS only; no customer audio. Internal harness validation.
  • n=4 is underpowered; a 20-call run is required before any confirmatory claim.
  • Batch queue instability observed; REST with chunking is the reliable transport. Calls over 30s are chunked, which can cut words and affect context — a confound.
  • Pilot audio was TTS; telephony processing exists in the repo but was not verified as applied here. WER is format-sensitive and not quoted as recognition quality.
